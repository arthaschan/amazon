FBA vs FBM Customer Service: What Sellers Actually Handle

One of these puts customer service on Amazon's plate. The other puts it entirely on yours. Here's the real division of labor — and where each model breaks.

By Tianyuan Chen · Updated August 2026

The Core Difference in One Paragraph

FBA (Fulfillment by Amazon) means Amazon stores, picks, packs, and ships your inventory — and handles most of the customer service that comes with fulfillment. FBM (Fulfillment by Merchant) means you do all of that yourself, including every buyer message.

This single distinction is the biggest driver of your customer service workload, and most new sellers don't fully grasp it until their inbox floods.

What FBA Sellers Actually Handle

With FBA, Amazon's customer service team handles:

  • Shipping and delivery status questions
  • Returns and refunds (via Amazon's Returns Center)
  • Damage and loss claims during fulfillment
  • General "where's my stuff" inquiries

What you still handle: product-specific questions via Buyer-Seller Messaging — sizing, compatibility, usage instructions, warranty details. These are usually a fraction of the total message volume.

FBA's hidden catch: Amazon's customer service optimizes for Amazon, not your brand. They may approve a return or refund faster than you'd like, and you have limited control over how your brand is represented. You trade workload for control.

What FBM Sellers Actually Handle

With FBM, you're on the hook for everything:

  • Every "where is my order?" (WISMO) message — and there are a lot
  • Tracking number distribution and delivery updates
  • Returns, refunds, and exchanges — end to end
  • Damage claims and delivery disputes
  • Product questions, complaints, and escalations

This is why FBM sellers are the ones who most urgently need automation. When WISMO makes up 60-70% of your messages and you're replying manually, you're paying a human to copy-paste tracking numbers all day.

How to Handle the FBM Workload Without Burning Out

If you're FBM (or hybrid), the answer isn't hiring more agents — it's automating the repetitive 80%. A self-hosted AI agent can:

  1. Answer WISMO instantly by pulling order status and tracking data
  2. Reply in 12 languages, 24/7, with no time-zone gap
  3. Handle return-policy questions from your documented policy
  4. Flag the 20% that genuinely need a human (disputes, A-to-z, angry buyers)

This turns the FBM support burden from "a second full-time job" into "10 minutes of review a day."

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I switch from FBA to FBM for lower fees?

Yes, but do the math first. FBM saves on FBA fees but shifts all customer service and logistics onto you. If you're not ready to handle (or automate) the message volume, the savings disappear into your own labor cost.

Does Amazon share buyer contact info with FBM sellers?

Amazon protects buyer privacy. FBM sellers communicate through Buyer-Seller Messaging, and Amazon provides shipping addresses only for fulfillment purposes. You never get the buyer's personal email.

Is hybrid (FBA + FBM) customer service complicated?

It's more complex because you're managing two support pipelines. A unified AI agent that handles both is the cleanest solution — it knows which orders are FBA (escalate to Amazon) and which are FBM (you answer).
